## 1. The observations the page rests on

- **Quasar polarisation alignment.** Hutsemékers and collaborators (2005, 2014) report that optical polarisation vectors of quasars are aligned over regions of order 1 Gpc, correlated with large-scale structure (`ESTABLISHED` as a published result; its significance and interpretation are debated and it has not been uniformly reproduced).
- **Radio-galaxy jet alignment.** Taylor & Jagannathan (2016) report jet axes aligned over about 100 Mpc in the ELAIS-N1 field.
- **Spin versus the cosmic web.** Tempel & Libeskind (2013) report a relation between galaxy spin and filament/sheet geometry; the direction (parallel or perpendicular) depends on galaxy type and mass, and the literature is mixed (the earlier page cited this as support for disks perpendicular to filaments; direction is to be rechecked at the source before it is used).

*The problem the page states:* tidal-torque theory (Peebles 1969) gives
angular-momentum correlations decaying over about 10 Mpc, so alignments over
hundreds of Mpc to Gpc are unexpected. Large-scale alignment signals of this
kind are an active research area, and that they require new physics is not the
consensus.

## 2. The model's explanation  `CONJECTURE`

A galaxy forming in a filament inherits the angular momentum of the local BEC
vortex field by direct coherent coupling: the filament is a vortex line, the
gas couples to its circulation, the spin axis aligns with the filament, and the
jet (along the spin axis) points along the filament. The correlation length is
the filament's length; "every galaxy on the same filament couples to the same
vortex line". The jet is read as a vortex-line *extension* that re-injects
angular momentum into the filament, so that filaments are maintained by AGN
activity ("turn off the AGN and the line decays"). In a superfluid vortex lines
cannot end in the bulk (they close into loops or end on boundaries); a
filament vortex line runs void to void.

Standing: a model. The jet-powers and circulation scales are not compared (the
open thread Γ = n·h/m versus jet power), and standard accounts of spin
alignment (tidal fields and mergers along filaments, with a mass-dependent spin
flip) are not excluded by anything here.

## 3. Geometry: jets along filaments, not along the wall normal

In the vortex lattice of [36](36_gravity_is_time_transversal.md): filaments are
vortex lines, sheets are where several meet, voids are vortex-free. Jets lie
along the vortex axis (the filament direction); at the Great Wall the filaments
lie in the wall plane, so jets should be parallel to the plane and rare along its
normal (a normal jet would need a vortex line running into a void).

## 4. The four predictions (all untested)

1. **Orientation near the Wall.** More than 80% of AGN jets near the Sloan Great Wall should be parallel to the wall plane; jets along the wall normal suppressed. Test: FIRST, NVSS, LoTSS orientations against the Wall's plane. The ">80%" threshold was written before any look and is kept as stated.
2. **Coherence length equals filament length**, filament by filament, not a universal scale. Test: do the Gpc alignments trace specific identified filaments?
3. **AGN duty cycle in filaments.** Filament AGN should be more active (or have higher jet power) than void AGN. Test: SDSS AGN activity against cosmic-web environment.
4. **Disk inclination against filament angle.** Disks perpendicular to filaments (spin along them). Test: extend Tempel & Libeskind to DESI and Rubin. Caveat of §1 on direction.

For each, a null model must be fixed beforehand: the expected jet-orientation
distribution given the filament geometry under standard physics (tidal alignment
exists, so some alignment is expected at the 10-Mpc scale), and the
significance threshold. Without it, any alignment fraction can be read as
support.

## 5. The SMIG link  `interpretation`

The cosmic web is read as the SMIG (supermassive inverted galaxy: the
zero-divisor variety at cosmic scale) projected from sedenion address space:
filaments as arms, voids as the pressure-maximum interior, clusters as arm tips;
the "84 zero-divisor arms" and the Lichtenberg-discharge picture, with AGN jets
as individual bolts. The 84 is the count of zero-divisors e_i ± e_j
([25](25_sedenion_manual.md) §4: exact). The cosmic web has no fixed number of
filaments, and matching its topology to 84 arms is an open thread; it is not a
