## 1. The references

- **[HerrmannManticaBessis1990]** H. J. Herrmann, G. Mantica & D. Bessis, *Space-filling bearings*, *Phys. Rev. Lett.* 65(26), 3223–3226 (1990) (verified): Apollonian-type packings in which every disk can rotate **without slipping** at every contact; proposed as a model for **low-dissipation hierarchical shear transmission** in turbulent boundary layers and in fault gouge.
- **[FrischParisi1985]** G. Parisi & U. Frisch, *On the singularity structure of fully developed turbulence*, in *Turbulence and Predictability in Geophysical Fluid Dynamics* (Varenna), 84–87 (1985): the **multifractal model**, turbulent dissipation concentrating on a spectrum of interwoven fractal sets (not machine-verified).
- **[FrischSulemNelkin1978]** U. Frisch, P.-L. Sulem & M. Nelkin, *A simple dynamical model of intermittent fully developed turbulence*, *J. Fluid Mech.* 87(4), 719–736 (1978) (verified): the **β-model**: the active eddies live on a recursively thinned Cantor set in space.
- **[Richardson1922]** L. F. Richardson, *Weather Prediction by Numerical Process* (CUP, 1922): "big whorls have little whorls …": the cascade as recursive gap-filling (not machine-verified).

## 2. What it anchors  `INTERPRETATION`

- **The Apollonian gasket is context flow** (not content storage): recursive gap-filling onto a sparse fractal skeleton, read as the eddy cascade and the multifractal dissipation set.
- **Eddies are document sections**; high Reynolds number (much context) means turbulent, and "the room fractures into recursively packed lobes".
- The bearing's low-dissipation shear hierarchy is read as the résumé/paragraph hierarchy passing "shear" (argument load) with minimal loss.

What is established: the cited models of turbulence and of bearings. What the framework adds is the identification of a *document's* section hierarchy with the gasket and the cascade; it is an analogy with no measurement attached, and it is distinct from the Navier–Stokes regularity question ([106](106_the_navier_stokes_problem.md), where the inertial-range remarks of §6 are not an argument for regularity).
