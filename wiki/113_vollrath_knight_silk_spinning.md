## 1. The references (checked)

- **[VollrathKnight2001]** F. Vollrath & D. P. Knight, *Liquid crystalline spinning of spider silk*, *Nature* 410, 541–548 (2001), doi:10.1038/35069000. The gland stores spidroin as a concentrated aqueous **dope**; the tapered duct applies, in sequence, ion exchange (Na⁺/Cl⁻ out, K⁺/PO₄/SO₄ in), acidification, water reabsorption and **elongational shear**; the dope passes through a **nematic liquid-crystalline** phase and the poly-alanine blocks nucleate β-sheet. Spinning is **pultrusion: drawing, not extrusion**.
- **[BlackledgeAgnarsson2010]** (the citation key keeps the earlier page's form; the authors are) I. Agnarsson, M. Kuntner & T. A. Blackledge, *Bioprospecting finds the toughest biological material: extraordinary silk from a giant riverine orb spider*, *PLoS ONE* 5(9), e11234 (2010): *Caerostris darwini* silk toughness (the earlier page: ~350–520 MJ/m³); the Veritasium test bench.
- **[Buehler2010]** (key as before; the authors are) S. Keten, Z. Xu, B. Ihle & M. J. Buehler, *Nanoconfinement controls stiffness, strength and mechanical toughness of β-sheet crystals in silk*, *Nature Materials* 9, 359–367 (2010): **2–3 nm** β-sheet nanocrystals in a semi-amorphous matrix; small crystals out-tough large ones.
