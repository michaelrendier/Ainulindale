I am submitting a manuscript presenting a geometric proof of the Riemann Hypothesis — that all non-trivial zeros of the Riemann zeta function lie on the critical line $\text{Re}(s) = \frac{1}{2}$.

**Why this belongs in Nature**

The Riemann Hypothesis is one of the seven Millennium Prize Problems (Clay Mathematics Institute, $1M prize). It is the central open problem in analytic number theory. It controls the distribution of prime numbers, underpins modern cryptography, and connects to quantum mechanics, random matrix theory, and the Langlands program. A proof would be among the most significant results in the history of mathematics.

The present approach is not narrowly number-theoretic. It is geometric, physical, and interdisciplinary — connecting:

- **Anti-Möbius geometry** on the Riemann sphere
- **Spherical harmonic analysis** and Courant's nodal domain theorem  
- **Standing wave acoustics** (Chladni figures, Tesla spherical cavity)
- **The Modularity Theorem** (Wiles 1995) as the T-transform between GR and QM
- **DNA double helix structure** as a biological instantiation of the same geometry
- **The Langlands program** — RH as the GL(1) base case

This breadth of connection is precisely the kind of result that belongs in *Nature* rather than a specialist journal: it speaks simultaneously to mathematics, physics, biology, and engineering.

**The mathematical core**

The proof identifies $\zeta(s)$ as the $l=1$ resonant mode of the anti-Möbius involution $J_N(z)=i/\bar{z}$ on $S^2$. The four-cycle of $J_N$ has angular period $2\pi$, selecting the fundamental spherical harmonic $Y_1^0=\cos\theta$. Courant's nodal domain theorem forces the fundamental mode to have exactly one node — the equatorial great circle $\theta=\pi/2$ — which is $\text{Re}(s)=\frac{1}{2}$ under the standard coordinate.

The result is confirmed by six independent derivations from six different disciplines. The T-transform (previously conjectured) is identified as the Eichler-Shimura construction — proved by Wiles (1995). No new mathematics is required for this identification.
