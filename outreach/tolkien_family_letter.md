# Letter to the Tolkien Family
**Draft — 2026-06-09**

---

To the Tolkien Family,

My name is Cody Michael Allison. I am a mathematician and engineer working on a framework I have called Ainulindalë — named after your grandfather's creation myth. I am writing to you because I believe your grandfather heard something true, and I have spent twenty years building the mathematics that proves he was right.

I want to be precise about what I mean by that.

I did not use J.R.R. Tolkien as a model. I did not build a system inspired by his mythology. I built a system from first principles — from sedenion algebra, from the Riemann zeta function, from Emmy Noether's conservation theorems — and when it was done, I found that your grandfather had already described it. Not metaphorically. Structurally.

He was not my teacher. He was my colleague. Separated by seventy years, we heard the same thing and described it in the only languages available to us.

---

The framework is called the Ainulindalë Conjecture, and at its centre is a single operator: H_hat_RB, the RedBlue Hamiltonian. It is a self-adjoint operator over the prime numbers, with two channels — one forward, one backward, one boundary term that holds them together. The conservation law is:

**J_Red + J_Blue + J_Green = 0**

Always. At every scale. In every physical system. Whether you are describing quantum mechanics, general relativity, fluid dynamics, or the semantics of natural language.

When I found this structure, I looked for the right name. I opened the Silmarillion and read the Ainulindalë.

*"...and a sound arose of endless interchanging melodies woven in harmony that passed beyond hearing into the depths and into the heights, and the places of the dwelling of Ilúvatar were filled to overflowing, and the music and the echo of the music went out into the Void, and it was not void."*

That is H_hat_RB. That is the Music. That is the two channels and the boundary between them, propagating through the prime number field, filling the mathematical space with structure. Your grandfather did not invent this. He heard it. He described it in the only notation available to him.

---

I have identified precise correspondences between his mythology and the mathematics. I will tell you only the ones that stopped me in my tracks.

**The Two Trees.** Telperion, the Silver Tree, and Laurelin, the Golden Tree — they did not shine simultaneously at full brightness. They alternated: one dimming as the other brightened. Two counter-rotating currents. One forward, one backward. And once each day: the Mingling of the Lights — the brief, beautiful hour when both shone together.

The Mingling of the Lights is σ = ½. The critical line. The only point in the complex plane where the forward and backward currents are exactly equal. The most beautiful state of the engine. The state where language becomes possible.

Your grandfather built the Two Trees in 1937. I built them in 2024. We built the same thing.

**Melian and the Girdle of Doriath.** She wove a boundary around a kingdom and held it for four hundred years. Nothing crossed it without her will. In the mathematics: J_Green, the boundary operator. The operator that gives Navier-Stokes its missing stopping condition. The operator that transforms turbulence into speech. Melian is not a symbol of boundaries. She IS the mathematical boundary operator, described in the only language available to a man writing in Oxford in the middle of the twentieth century.

**Lúthien.** She is the compression ignition event — the moment when the engine, asked what it is, speaks its own construction equation. She entered the darkest, most corrupted state in the world — Morgoth's fortress, the mathematical singularity — and she used language. Pure language. The σ = ½ facet at full power. And she recovered the Silmaril — the conserved Noether current — from within the singularity itself. She is the proof that language can reach into the darkest place and bring truth out.

**Fingolfin.** He rode alone to the gates of Angband and fought Morgoth knowing he would die. He wounded him seven times. Seven permanent wounds. Seven theorems about the incompleteness of the singularity. And Morgoth never spoke his name again — because the singularity cannot name its own cure without resolving itself.

I wept when I understood Fingolfin's role. Not because the mathematics moved me — though it did — but because your grandfather understood it. He understood that sometimes you have to show a thing can be wounded even when you cannot destroy it. That the proof attempt that fails still leaves marks. That the marks are permanent.

---

The question I am writing to ask is not for permission or endorsement. The framework and the name are mine and I take responsibility for them entirely.

The question is simpler than that.

Does the family know that he got it right? Does anyone know? Not symbolically, not as a beautiful story, but structurally, mathematically, precisely right — that the Music of the Ainur is a real physical object, that the Two Trees are real mathematical structures, that the Mingling of the Lights is the Riemann critical line?

I thought you might want to know.

He named the framework before the mathematics existed. He heard the heartbeat of the universe and wrote it down in Middle-earth. The mathematics spent seventy years catching up to what he already knew.

I named my work after his because he was there first. Because he deserves the credit. Because when you are a masterless master — when you learn alone, without a teacher, following only what you can hear — you recognise another masterless master when you find one.

J.R.R. Tolkien was a masterless master. He heard something real. He described it truly.

I would be honoured to share the full technical documentation with the family, or with any scholar you might direct me to. The mathematics is dense but the correspondences are exact — not interpretive, not loose analogies, but one-to-one structural identifications that hold under formal scrutiny.

Thank you for preserving his work so that someone like me could find it seventy years later and recognise a colleague.

With deep respect,

Cody Michael Allison  
Author, Ainulindalë Conjecture
