# Post-Stroke Right-Side Recovery — What to Ask For

**Prepared 2026-08-17.** For a stroke survivor in a long-term care facility in
Jefferson Parish, Louisiana, dual-eligible (Medicare + Medicaid), with right-side
weakness.

> **This is not medical advice and it is not an eligibility determination.** It is a
> preparation document: the correct terminology, the specific named interventions, the
> facts her clinicians will need, and the coverage rule that facilities most often get
> wrong. Every item here is a question to put to her care team. They decide.

---

## 0. The single most important item on this page

**Jimmo v. Sebelius.** If anyone says she has *plateaued*, has *no rehab potential*, or
*isn't improving, so therapy stops* — **that is not a lawful basis for a Medicare denial.**

The 2013 nationwide settlement (Jimmo v. Sebelius, No. 11-cv-17, D. Vt.) eliminated the
so-called "Improvement Standard." CMS's own manual now states that skilled nursing and
skilled therapy are covered **to maintain the patient's current condition or to prevent
or slow decline**, and that coverage **cannot be denied for absence of improvement
potential**. A patient does not have to be getting better, and does not have to
deteriorate first, to qualify.

What to do with it:

- Print `CMS_Jimmo_factsheet.pdf` and `CMS_Jimmo_program_manual.pdf` (both in this folder,
  both official CMS documents) and put them in her chart.
- Ask that the therapy plan of care be written with **explicit maintenance goals** if
  restorative goals are no longer the basis. That is the specific language that keeps it
  covered.
- Facilities apply the old standard constantly, usually not maliciously — they were
  trained on it. A calm reference to the CMS fact sheet resolves most of it.

## 1. Facts to establish first — these gate everything else

Get these written down before any referral conversation. The first one is a hard gate.

1. **Was the stroke ISCHEMIC or HEMORRHAGIC?**
   The FDA-approved paired-VNS device (below) is indicated **only for ischemic** stroke.
   If the stroke was a bleed, that option is off the table and the others still apply.
2. **Date of the stroke.** "Chronic" generally means ≥6 months; the pivotal trial enrolled
   at ≥9 months.
3. **Current Fugl-Meyer Assessment — Upper Extremity (FMA-UE) score.** This is the standard
   motor measure and the one eligibility is written against. If no one has scored it, that
   is itself worth asking for.
4. **Does she have any active wrist or finger extension?** Even a little changes which
   therapies are available.
5. **Modified Ashworth Scale score** (spasticity/tone) for the affected arm.
6. **Any prior vagotomy?** A bilateral or left cervical vagotomy is the listed
   contraindication for the VNS device.

## 2. The referral to ask for by name

**"I would like a Physical Medicine & Rehabilitation (PM&R / physiatry) consult for
chronic stroke motor recovery."**

This is the highest-value single request on the page. A physiatrist is the specialist who
coordinates post-stroke motor recovery, orders the neuromodulation work-ups, and manages
spasticity. Primary care and general neurology often will not initiate these on their own.

Ochsner Medical Center's main campus is at 1514 Jefferson Highway, **in Jefferson, LA**, and
Ochsner is a certified Comprehensive Stroke Center — so a major neuroscience service is
geographically close. (Whether they specifically implant the VNS device is **not
confirmed** — ask directly, and see §3.)

## 3. Named interventions, strongest evidence first

### a. Vivistim Paired VNS — FDA-approved, not experimental

The only FDA-approved device therapy for this indication. **CPT 64568.**

- **FDA indication, verbatim from the approval record (P210007):** *"intended to be used to
  stimulate the vagus nerve during rehabilitation therapy in order to reduce upper extremity
  motor deficits and improve motor function in chronic ischemic stroke patients with
  moderate to severe arm impairment."*
- **Evidence:** VNS-REHAB, a randomised, blinded, sham-controlled pivotal trial. A clinically
  meaningful FMA-UE response at 90 days was achieved in **47% of the VNS group vs 24% of
  controls**. No unexpected serious device-related adverse events.
- **What it involves:** surgical implantation, then ~6 weeks of in-clinic paired therapy
  (3×/week, 18 sessions), then a paired home program. It is *stimulation paired with
  therapy* — the therapy is not optional, it is the mechanism.
- **Medicare:** covered under New Technology APC 1580, national average ~$45,000 for the
  outpatient procedure, maintained in the CY2027 proposed rule. `Vivistim_2026_Rehab_Coding_Guide.pdf`
  in this folder has the rehabilitation-side billing codes her therapy provider will need.
- **Ask:** *"Am I a candidate for Vivistim paired VNS, and if you don't implant it here, who
  is the nearest certified center?"* Use the manufacturer's center locator at vivistim.com
  as a cross-check.

### b. Functional Electrical Stimulation (FES / NMES)

Far lower barrier — surface electrodes, no surgery, widely available in rehab settings.

- **Contralaterally controlled FES (CCFES)** — the stronger variant, where her *unaffected*
  hand controls the stimulation to the affected one. Meta-analysis favours it over
  conventional NMES (`CCFES_vs_NMES_metaanalysis_PMC10915254.pdf`).
- **BCI-controlled FES** — moderate effect on upper limb function, SMD 0.50 (95% CI
  0.26–0.73), 10 RCTs, 290 patients (`BCI_FES_metaanalysis_PMC11464471.pdf`).
- General FES evidence base: `FES_upper_limb_systematic_review_FrontNeurol2023_PMC10739305.pdf`
  and the network meta-analysis `Electrical_Magnetic_Stim_UE_network_metaanalysis_PMC12345400.pdf`.
- **Ask:** *"Can we add FES — ideally contralaterally controlled — to her OT plan?"*

### c. Spasticity management — frequently under-offered

If tone is limiting her arm, **botulinum toxin injection** is standard, covered under
Medicare Part B, and routinely not raised in LTC settings. It does not itself restore
strength, but it can unmask movement that tone was hiding, and it protects range of motion.

**Ask:** *"Is she a candidate for botulinum toxin for upper-limb spasticity, and can we
re-assess her Ashworth score?"*

### d. Constraint-Induced Movement Therapy (CIMT)

Behavioural, no device, strong evidence base — but requires some active wrist/finger
extension (see §1.4). Often delivered as modified CIMT (mCIMT) in lower-intensity settings.

### e. High-dose task-specific repetitive practice

Dose is the thing that most consistently predicts motor gain. In an LTC facility the
realistic constraint is therapy *minutes*, not therapy *type* — which is exactly why §0
matters more than anything in §3.

### f. If she has an exoskeleton or powered orthosis

A device is not a parallel option to the therapies above — it **multiplies** them, because
it raises the dose of task-specific practice she can actually perform. It is also the
natural therapy partner for paired VNS or FES, where stimulation has to be paired with
*activity* to do anything at all.

**⚠ The safety item that matters most: SKIN.**

This is the one to take seriously above every other line on this page. A person with
impaired sensation on the affected side **cannot feel a pressure injury developing**. She
cannot signal that a strap is too tight, and sustained strap pressure restricts blood flow.
The documented progression is skin ulcer → necrotic tissue → infection. Christopher Reeve
survived nine years of C1/C2 tetraplegia, recovered sensation and some voluntary movement,
and died of sepsis from an infected pressure ulcer.

Non-negotiables:

- **Skin checks at every contact point after every single use.** Redness, rubbing,
  indentation that doesn't fade. Written into the care plan, not left to memory.
- **Trained staff apply and remove it**, at least until fit is confirmed — proper alignment
  is the whole difference between a therapeutic device and a pressure source.
- **An OT fits it.** An unfitted orthosis on an insensate limb is a hazard, not a treatment.

**Recovery vs compensation — ask which one this device is doing.**

The distinction is worth raising explicitly with her therapists. Devices that require *her
own* effort — EMG-triggered, or assist-as-needed — recruit her volitional drive and are the
ones aligned with neuroplasticity. Devices that move the limb *for* her substitute for that
drive. Both have legitimate uses (function now vs recovery later), but they are different
goals and should be a deliberate choice rather than an accident of which device arrived.

**If she needs a powered, EMG-driven one, there is a Medicare-covered path.** The MyoPro
powered elbow/hand orthosis reads her own EMG through surface sensors, so it only moves when
she tries to move — she **must** be able to generate detectable EMG in that arm, which is
itself worth testing. It is covered under **Medicare Part B** as DMEPOS, codes **L8701** and
**L8702** (CMS fee schedule rates $33,480.90 and $65,871.74, effective April 2024).

**Bonus, and not a small one:** a fitted device with documented functional activity
*strengthens* the §0 argument. It is concrete evidence that skilled therapy is producing or
maintaining function — exactly what a maintenance-goal plan of care needs on the record.

**Ask:** *"Can OT fit and document this device in her plan of care, establish a skin-check
protocol at every contact point, and tell me whether it's assisting her own effort or
replacing it?"*

## 4. What is deliberately NOT in this document

Per Cody's scope: no spinal-cord-injury material, no passive-transport-across-injury work,
no epidural stimulation. Stroke and SCI are different literatures with different
indications and should not be blended in a document someone may hand to a clinician.

---

## 5. The order to actually do this in

1. Call **SHIIP (1-800-259-5301)** — free, and they know Louisiana dual-eligible rules cold.
2. Put the **Jimmo** fact sheets in her chart; get maintenance goals written into the plan of care.
3. Request the **PM&R consult** in writing.
4. Establish **ischemic vs haemorrhagic** and get an **FMA-UE score**.
5. Raise **FES** and **spasticity management** with the current team — these can start now.
6. Raise **Vivistim** candidacy with PM&R, given ischemic + chronic + moderate-to-severe.
7. If therapy is cut or refused on "plateau" grounds, call the **Ombudsman (866) 632-0922**.
