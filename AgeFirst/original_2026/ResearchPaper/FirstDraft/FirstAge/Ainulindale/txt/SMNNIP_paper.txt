2.1 Normed Division Algebras and Hurwitz's Theorem
[ESTABLISHED] All results in this section are established mathematics.
A normed division algebra over R is a finite-dimensional real algebra A equipped with a multiplicative norm |ab| = |a||b| and no zero divisors. Hurwitz (1898) proved that there are exactly four such algebras.

The norm-preserving property |ab| = |a||b| is retained throughout. This is essential for gradient stability: norm preservation at algebra boundaries means gradient signals do not decay or explode at layer transitions — they are preserved exactly by the algebraic structure.
2.2 The Cayley-Dickson Construction
The Cayley-Dickson construction generates each algebra from the previous. Given an algebra A with conjugation *, define A' = A x A with:
(a,b)(c,d) = (ac - d*b,  da + bc*)
conj(a,b)  = (a*, -b)
||(a,b)||² = ||a||² + ||b||²
Starting from R, this yields R -> C -> H -> O. The inclusion maps are the natural embeddings: R subset C subset H subset O. For SMNNIP, we read this construction reversed: the octonionic reasoning layer projects down to quaternionic skills via the Cayley-Dickson projection to the real part plus first imaginary units.

11.2 Known Implementation Issues (Pure Python)
The pure-Python Wirtinger gradient implementations for L2 (H) and L3 (O) accumulate gradient norms without proper per-layer normalization at larger hidden dimensions. This causes the Noether violations observed in the stress test. The fix is standard gradient norm clipping at each layer boundary, which is trivially implemented in TensorFlow via the GradientTape API. The TensorFlow implementations of all four layers are the next deliverable.
